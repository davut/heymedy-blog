There are more AI health apps right now than at any point in history. That should be good news. But if you've tried to pick one lately, you know it mostly feels like standing in a cereal aisle that stretches forever — dozens of boxes, each promising something slightly different, none making it easy to figure out which one is actually right for you.

The truth is, "health tracking" means very different things to different people. Maybe you want to log symptoms across a couple of chronic conditions. Maybe you want an AI that can read your lab results and explain what's going on in plain English. Maybe you just want to track medications and not miss a dose. The best app for you depends entirely on what problem you're trying to solve.

So instead of ranking these apps from one to five and calling it a day, here's an honest look at what each one does well — and where it falls short.

## Bearable: Best for Symptom and Mood Correlation

Bearable has built a loyal following among people managing chronic conditions, and it's easy to see why. The app lets you track symptoms, mood, energy, sleep, and medications with just a few taps, and then generates correlation reports that surface patterns you might never notice on your own. ("Every time my sleep drops below six hours, my pain spikes two days later" — that kind of insight.)

The free tier is generous: unlimited custom symptoms, weekly reports, and a customizable graph. The premium subscription runs about $35 a year and unlocks deeper correlation reports and unlimited history. With a 4.8-star rating on the App Store from thousands of reviews, it's clearly resonating.

Where Bearable is less strong is in the medical records department. It's built around self-reported data — what you log each day — rather than pulling in lab results, doctor's notes, or clinical documents. If your main goal is tracking symptoms and finding patterns, it's excellent. If you want a single place for your entire health picture, you may need to pair it with something else.

## CareClinic: Best for Structured Care Plans

CareClinic takes a more clinical approach. It offers evidence-based care plans for over 100 conditions, built with protocols from institutions like Stanford and Mayo Clinic. You can track medications, symptoms, measurements, and nutrition, and the app syncs data from Apple Health, glucose meters, and blood pressure monitors.

A recent update added health document storage — you can upload and organize lab reports, prescriptions, and other records directly in the app. It also includes standardized assessments like PHQ-9 and GAD-7, which are the same mental health screening tools used in clinical settings.

The trade-off is complexity. CareClinic has a lot of features, and some users report that the learning curve can feel steep. Pricing has also been a point of confusion — the app offers free core tracking, but premium features require a subscription. If you're managing a specific chronic condition and want a structured, protocol-driven approach, CareClinic is worth exploring. If you want something lighter, it might feel like more than you need.

## ChatGPT Health: Best for On-Demand Health Questions

OpenAI launched ChatGPT Health in early 2026, and it represents a fundamentally different approach. Rather than being a daily tracking app, it's a conversational AI that can connect to your medical records (through a partnership with b.well) and wellness apps like Apple Health and MyFitnessPal.

The appeal is obvious: you can ask questions in plain language — "What did my last blood panel show?" or "How should I prepare for my cardiology appointment?" — and get answers grounded in your actual health data. OpenAI says 230 million users were already asking health questions each week before the dedicated Health feature even launched.

The limitations are worth noting. ChatGPT Health is primarily a question-and-answer tool, not a daily tracker. It doesn't send medication reminders. It doesn't generate symptom correlation reports. And the medical records integration is currently US-only, with availability rolling out gradually. Privacy-conscious users should also weigh the implications of sharing health records with a general-purpose AI platform, even one with dedicated data protections.

## Apple Health (and the Coming Health+): Best for the Apple Ecosystem

Apple Health is already on every iPhone, quietly collecting steps, heart rate, sleep data, and more from your Apple Watch and connected devices. It's the default health dashboard for hundreds of millions of people.

What's changing in 2026 is the AI layer. Apple is preparing to launch Health+, a service that would add personalized coaching, nutrition tracking, and AI-generated health recommendations based on your data. Reports suggest it will include meal tracking, expert video content, and eventually integration with a redesigned Siri for health-related questions. Perplexity and ChatGPT have also announced Apple Health integrations.

The strength of Apple Health is that it's already there, already collecting data, and deeply integrated into the hardware you carry. The weakness, at least today, is that it's still primarily a data repository. Until Health+ officially launches, the app itself doesn't do much to help you interpret or act on what it collects. And if you're not fully in the Apple ecosystem, you're mostly out of luck.

## heymedy: Best for Pulling Your Whole Health Picture Together

heymedy approaches the problem differently. Rather than focusing on one type of tracking, it's designed as an AI health companion that brings medications, symptoms, health records, and personal context into one place — and then helps you make sense of all of it.

You can log medications and symptoms, upload health documents, and ask the AI questions about your own health data in plain language. The idea is that your health isn't a series of isolated data points — it's a story, and an AI that knows the full context can help you understand it better than one that only sees a piece.

heymedy is still a newer app, which means it's building its feature set and community. It doesn't have the decade-long track record of some competitors, and it's currently iOS-only. But if what you've been looking for is a single, warm, easy-to-use place where your medications, symptoms, labs, and questions all live together — with an AI that actually remembers your context — it's worth trying.

## So Which One Should You Choose?

There's no single "best" app here. The right choice depends on your situation:

If you're managing chronic symptoms and want to find patterns, **Bearable** is hard to beat. If you want structured care plans built around clinical protocols, **CareClinic** offers real depth. If you mainly want to ask health questions and already have records in a connected system, **ChatGPT Health** is powerful. If you're deep in the Apple ecosystem, **Apple Health** (and soon Health+) will keep getting smarter. And if you want one place where your medications, symptoms, records, and AI questions all come together, **heymedy** is built for exactly that.

The most important thing isn't which app you pick. It's that you stop letting your health data sit untouched in apps you never open. Pick one that fits your life, give it a real try for a couple of weeks, and see if it changes how you understand your own health. You might be surprised.
