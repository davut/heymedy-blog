You've decided to actually track your symptoms. Maybe a new diagnosis made it urgent. Maybe you're tired of walking into appointments and trying to reconstruct weeks of pain, fatigue, or brain fog from memory. Either way, you're looking for an app — and you've probably landed on two names: Bearable and heymedy.

Both help you log what's happening in your body. But they approach the problem from very different angles, and picking the right one depends on what you're really trying to get out of tracking in the first place.

## What Bearable Does Well

Bearable has been around since 2019 and has built a devoted community, particularly among people managing chronic illness. It carries a 4.8-star rating on the App Store and 4.7 stars on Google Play from thousands of reviews — numbers that are hard to earn and harder to keep.

The app is built around one powerful idea: if you log enough data consistently, patterns will emerge. You record symptoms, mood, energy, sleep, medications, and whatever custom factors matter to you. After about a week of logging, Bearable starts generating correlation reports — surfacing connections like "your fatigue scores are worse on days you skip your evening walk" or "your pain tends to spike two days after poor sleep."

The free tier is genuinely usable. You get unlimited custom symptoms, weekly reports, and a customizable graph. Premium runs about $35 a year (often discounted) and unlocks deeper correlation analysis, unlimited history, and additional report types. It syncs with Apple Health and Google Fit, so steps, sleep, and other wearable data flow in automatically.

Where Bearable is strongest is in helping you see your own patterns over time. For people with conditions like fibromyalgia, endometriosis, IBS, or ME/CFS — where day-to-day fluctuations matter enormously and triggers can be subtle — that kind of insight is genuinely valuable.

## What heymedy Does Differently

heymedy takes a broader approach. Rather than focusing primarily on symptom logging and correlations, it's designed as an AI health companion that brings your medications, symptoms, health records, and personal context into a single place — and then helps you understand all of it through conversation.

The core difference is the AI layer. You can ask heymedy questions about your own health data in plain language: "Have my headaches improved since I started the new medication?" or "What patterns should I mention at my next appointment?" Because the AI has access to everything you've logged — medications, symptoms, uploaded lab results, doctor's notes — it can give answers grounded in your actual health story rather than generic information.

heymedy also handles health document storage. You can upload lab results, prescriptions, and clinical notes, so your health picture lives in one app instead of scattered across patient portals, photo albums, and email attachments. Medication tracking, symptom logging, and document storage all feed into the same system the AI draws from.

heymedy is a newer app and currently iOS-only. It doesn't have years of community-tested correlation algorithms like Bearable does. But if what frustrates you most is that your health data is fragmented across five different places — and none of them talk to each other — heymedy is built to solve that specific problem.

## Where They Overlap — and Where They Don't

Both apps let you log symptoms and medications. Both sync with Apple Health. Both take privacy seriously. But the overlap mostly ends there.

Bearable is a specialist. It's optimized for one thing — helping you track as many health variables as possible and then finding the connections between them. It does this very well, and its correlation engine is genuinely its standout feature. If you're the kind of person who wants to log 15 different metrics every day and then geek out over the weekly report, Bearable is hard to beat.

heymedy is a generalist with an AI brain. It's less focused on high-volume daily logging and more focused on giving you a single place where your whole health picture lives — medications, symptoms, records, questions — and an AI that can help you make sense of it. It won't generate a statistical correlation report the way Bearable does, but it will let you ask "What's been going on with my migraines this month?" and get a contextual answer based on everything you've logged and uploaded.

Here's a quick comparison:

| | Bearable | heymedy |
|---|---|---|
| Symptom tracking | Deep, with custom metrics | Yes, alongside meds and records |
| Correlation reports | Statistical, pattern-based | AI-driven, conversational |
| Medication tracking | Basic logging and reminders | Logging, reminders, context-aware |
| Health records storage | No | Yes (labs, notes, prescriptions) |
| AI health questions | No | Yes, grounded in your data |
| Wearable sync | Apple Health + Google Fit | Apple Health |
| Platform | iOS + Android | iOS only |
| Free tier | Generous | Core features |
| Premium pricing | ~$35/year | Core features free |
| Best for | Finding patterns in symptoms | Pulling your health picture together |

## Which One Fits Your Life?

If you're managing a chronic condition and the thing you need most is to find patterns — to figure out what makes your symptoms better or worse — Bearable has years of refinement behind that specific mission. Its correlation engine is the real deal, and the community of people using it for conditions like chronic fatigue, chronic pain, and mood disorders is large and active.

If your frustration is more about fragmentation — medications tracked in one place, lab results in a patient portal, symptoms in your Notes app, and no single tool that ties it all together — heymedy is designed for exactly that. The AI layer means you don't have to be the one connecting the dots; you can just ask.

Some people will use both, at least for a while. Track daily symptoms in Bearable for the correlation reports, and keep heymedy as the place where your health records, medication list, and big-picture questions live. There's nothing wrong with that.

The more important decision isn't which app you pick. It's whether you start tracking at all. A month of consistent data — in either app — will teach you more about your body than a year of trying to remember.
