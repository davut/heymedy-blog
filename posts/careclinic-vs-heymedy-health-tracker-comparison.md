You've decided to take your health tracking seriously. Maybe you're juggling medications, logging symptoms, or just trying to walk into your next doctor's appointment with something more useful than "I've been feeling kinda off." You open the App Store, type in "health tracker," and now you're staring at dozens of options that all promise to organize your health life.

Two names that come up a lot in 2026 are CareClinic and heymedy. Both use AI. Both let you track medications and symptoms. But they approach the problem differently — and depending on what you actually need, one might fit your routine a lot better than the other.

Here's an honest look at both.

## CareClinic: The Detailed Self-Care Planner

CareClinic has been around for a while and has built a reputation as a thorough health tracking app. It's available on iOS, Android, and the web, and it's designed for people who want to log just about everything — medications, symptoms, nutrition, physical activity, therapy sessions, mood, and more.

**What it does well:**

CareClinic's strongest feature is its care plan system. You can create separate plans for different conditions or combine everything into one. The app supports tracking over 3,500 conditions, and its AI adapts to show you relevant trackers based on what you're managing. After about two weeks of logging, it starts surfacing correlations — showing you which factors seem to worsen or improve your symptoms.

The medication tracking is solid. You can set personalized reminders, log doses, and track refills. CareClinic claims users with notifications enabled reach 95% medication adherence. It also integrates with Apple Health and Google Fit, and lets you share care plans with family members or caregivers.

The app has a 4.3-star rating on the App Store with over 2,000 ratings.

**Where it gets tricky:**

CareClinic uses a freemium model. The core logging features are free, but many of the more useful tools — advanced analytics, PDF reports, caregiver sharing — sit behind a premium subscription starting around $4.99/month (or roughly $60/year for an annual plan). Some users have noted that the paywall appears frequently as you explore the app, which can make it hard to evaluate what you're getting before you commit.

The depth of CareClinic is also its trade-off. If you want to track everything in fine detail, it delivers. But if you're looking for something you can use quickly without a lot of setup, the number of options and screens can feel overwhelming at first.

## heymedy: The AI Health Companion

heymedy takes a different approach. Rather than giving you a dashboard full of trackers to configure, it leads with conversation. You can talk to an AI assistant that understands your health context — your medications, symptoms, conditions, and records — and ask it questions in plain language.

**What it does well:**

heymedy is built around the idea that your health data is only useful if you can actually make sense of it. You can log medications and symptoms, but you can also upload health records, lab results, and documents. The AI reads them and can answer questions like "what did my last blood test say about my cholesterol?" or "are any of my medications known to interact?"

This is where the broader 2026 trend matters. Apps like Fitbit, ChatGPT Health, and Microsoft Copilot Health are all racing to connect medical records with AI assistants. heymedy was designed around this idea from the start — your health context lives in one place, and the AI uses it to give you answers that are actually about *you*, not generic search results.

heymedy is currently iOS-only, and it's a newer app with a smaller user base than CareClinic. It doesn't have the same breadth of manual tracking options — you won't find a body symptom mapper or nutrition tracker built in. What it offers instead is speed and simplicity: a single place to store your health information and an AI that helps you understand it.

**Where it's still growing:**

heymedy is earlier in its journey. It doesn't yet have the years of feature depth that CareClinic has built up, and it's only available on iPhone for now. If you need Android or web access, that's a dealbreaker today. The app is also more focused on the AI conversation experience, so if you prefer structured dashboards and manual logging over chatting with an AI, it may not match your style.

## So Which One Should You Pick?

It depends on what kind of health tracker you actually are.

If you're the type who wants to log every dose, every symptom severity score, and every meal — and you enjoy reviewing charts and correlations — CareClinic gives you the tools to do that across multiple platforms. It's mature, feature-rich, and works well for people managing complex or chronic conditions who want granular control.

If you're the type who collects health documents in a drawer (or a camera roll), forgets what your doctor said about that lab result, and wants to just *ask* an AI what's going on — heymedy is designed for that. It's less about filling out trackers and more about having your health information in one place where it's actually useful.

Neither app is perfect, and neither replaces your doctor. They're tools that help you show up to your appointments better prepared and stay on top of your health between visits.

The good news is both have free options, so you can try each one and see which fits.
